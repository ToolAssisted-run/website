Claim your identity

To protect the identity of authors from other TAS sites, their names are held for them here: nobody else can take one. Claiming links yours to your account, hands you the held username, and lets you bring your own runs over.

Ask here, and the Steering Committee answers. Say what shows the name is yours: a post from the account that name belongs to, a channel hosting your encodes, an account we have known for years. The Committee alone assesses identity (Terms 4.8): no expert, no moderator, nobody else may hand a held name to anybody. You are told either way, and the answer is public in the site log.

There is a second route to the same judges: a Committee member may attest an identity they can vouch for directly, naming themselves and what they checked. That is their judgement on the record rather than a claim you filed, and it is logged separately.

An obfuscated form of your email address is shown to the Steering Committee while your claim is open. Not the whole address: johndoe@email.com reaches them as jo***oe@e****.com. It is enough for somebody to tell whether it is the address that author would have, which is the point of it, and not enough to write to you or to work out who you are from nothing. (Privacy 6.4.1.) Neither form is written into the archive, shown on the site, or kept anywhere: it is worked out at the moment they look. Filing a claim is how you agree to that; if you would rather not, ask on the forum instead and a site-wide expert can attest it without a claim being filed here.

A ban elsewhere is not a status here. It neither blocks a claim nor follows you (Principles 1.4, 1.5).

Once claimed, the username is yours. If the name comes from a site we can read from, your profile also grows an Import my runs button. You pick which of your publications come over, co-authored ones included; importing a co-authored work is your responsibility (Terms 3.7).
