Multiple author submission policy

What submitting or importing a collaborative work means here, and whose call it is.

Many TAS works are collaborative: several authors put their time and effort into one movie. When you submit such a work here, you must:

  1. be one of its authors;
  2. credit every co-author accurately, as the site's CC BY 4.0 license and the Terms of Use require;
  3. if the work was first published elsewhere, make sure that source licenses it for free redistribution, and credit the authors named there according to that license.

Consent of co-authors

Anyone may copy and redistribute a TAS work published under a CC license; that is what the license is for. Your co-authors may still expect to be asked before their work is republished. We therefore strongly encourage you to ask every co-author before submitting. toolAssisted.run does not require their consent, and a co-author keeps the right to distribute the movie file even if the others refuse. But if you submit without consent, the consequences of that choice are yours alone.

Submitting without consent is understandable when:

  • you did most of the work, and the other contributions were small touch-ups;
  • a co-author cannot be reached, for instance because they have not been seen online for a long time;
  • a co-author refuses contact or does not answer, so consent cannot even be asked for.

Think twice before submitting without consent when:

  • you and your co-authors contributed roughly equally;
  • you are a minor contributor, and most of the work is someone else's.

What may never be submitted

  • A work redistributed from a copyrighted, licensed, or otherwise not freely redistributable source.
  • A work that contains or encodes copyrighted material (for example, inputs that reproduce a protected work of art).
  • A work that contains or encodes illegal or pornographic material.
  • A work that breaks the site's rules.

To restate the site's position: ask your co-authors. If you choose not to, you answer for whatever follows. If a co-author objects after the fact, any author can have the run withdrawn, and all authors together can have it permanently erased (Principles).
